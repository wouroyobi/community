Why This Archive Exists

A community that remembers itself, on its own terms

Most accounts of Mbororo life have been written by outsiders — travellers, administrators, researchers passing through. Wouro Yobi is writing its own. This archive is the community's attempt to hold its history, knowledge and daily life in one place, told in its own voice and kept for the people who will need it after us.

What you will find here is not a brochure. It is a working record — of the elders who carry oral history, the leaders who carry decisions, the herders and farmers who carry the land, and the children who will carry all of it forward. Every entry is built to connect to another: a person to a project, a project to a place, a place to a story still being written.

We preserve what came before so that what comes next is built with memory intact.

Wouro Yobi Living Archive — established as a community-led initiative

Community Spotlight

Ardo Abdoulkadi

Ardo Abdoulkadi leads the Mbororo Social and Cultural Development Association (MBOSCUDA) for the Mayo-Banyo division, representing the interests of Mbororo communities across land rights, education access and pastoral development. His work connects Wouro Yobi to a wider regional network of Mbororo advocacy and self-representation.

Within the community, his leadership has shaped decisions on schooling, infrastructure requests to government partners, and the day-to-day mediation that keeps a pastoral community's many interests aligned.

"A community that documents itself is a community that cannot be spoken over."

Our Memory

What the elders carry, we are learning to keep

Songs sung at gatherings, proverbs passed down in everyday speech, and the recollection of elders who watched Wouro Yobi grow — this is the most fragile part of the archive, and the most urgent to record. A community timeline is being built interview by interview.

Archive Methodology

Nothing here exists without evidence

Every story published to this archive is built to carry the material that proves it — so a claim about the past or present can always be traced back to its source.

PhotographsDated and attributed to a place and event.
VideosFootage of events and everyday life.
InterviewsFirst-person testimony, recorded and transcribed.
MapsLocations tied to coordinates and boundaries.
DocumentsOfficial records and correspondence.
ReportsFindings and progress summaries.
TimelinesEvents placed in chronological sequence.
Community VoicesTestimony gathered directly, in context.

Every published story links to at least one primary source — a photograph, document, recording or dated event — before it is considered complete. This is the standard the archive holds itself to.
