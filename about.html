Why This Archive Exists

Written from within the community, not about it

Most of what has ever been written about communities like Wouro Yobi was written by someone passing through — a researcher, an administrator, a visitor with a notebook and a limited amount of time. What gets left behind is often accurate in fact but foreign in perspective.

Oral history disappears quietly. An elder who dies without being recorded takes a version of the community's story with them that cannot be recovered. Local knowledge — how to read the land, how disputes are resolved, why a place is named what it's named — rarely survives outside the people who hold it, unless someone deliberately writes it down.

The Living Archive exists because future generations deserve access to their own history, told by the people who lived it, verified as carefully as any institutional archive would verify its own record — and written from within the community, rather than about the community from a distance.

How We Work

Editorial principles

Every page in this archive is held to the same eight standards.

Community-Led

Content originates from and is verified by the community itself.

Evidence-Based

Claims are supported by photographs, documents, interviews or records.

Respect

People, culture and belief are presented with dignity, never spectacle.

Transparency

Sources, methods and open questions are stated plainly, not hidden.

Accuracy

Unverified information is marked as such rather than presented as fact.

Continuous Learning

Pages are revised as new evidence and interviews become available.

Safeguarding

Sensitive information and imagery of children are handled with care.

Long-Term Preservation

The archive is built to remain accessible for decades, not one campaign.

In Brief

About Wouro Yobi

Wouro Yobi is a Mbororo community in the Mayo-Darlé Municipality, Adamawa Region, Cameroon — a highland savanna landscape shaped by pastoral life. This page exists to explain the archive itself; the community's fuller story, leadership, geography and traditions are documented in depth elsewhere in the archive.

Governance

Community administration

Wouro Yobi combines traditional leadership, community participation and collaboration with public institutions.

Traditional Leadership — His Majesty Maître Aliou Amadou

Traditional leadership guides custom, mediates disputes and represents the community in dealings with visiting officials and partners. A fuller leadership profile, including his stated vision for the community's future, is documented elsewhere in the archive.

Leadership Profile

The Chief's Camp

The Chief's Camp serves as an important place for community leadership, dialogue, and the reception of visitors and delegations. It is where much of the community's discussion and decision-making takes place. Further documentation — its history, its role in daily governance, and photographs — will be added to the archive over time as it is gathered.

Community Development

Documenting
Did you know?
Community-led digital archives are still rare across rural Africa. Most digital records of pastoral communities like the Mbororo have historically been produced by outside researchers or institutions — which is part of why the Living Archive's community-authored approach matters.
